Affiliate marketing is simple on paper. You recommend a product, someone buys it through your link, and the company pays you a cut. No inventory, no customer service, no product to build.

That basic deal hasn't changed. What changed in 2026 is almost everything around it: how people find recommendations, how much the biggest programs pay, and what Google is willing to rank. If you learned affiliate marketing from a video made a few years ago, some of that advice will now work against you.

Here's what's different going into 2027, what still works, and how we'd start today.

## What changed in 2026

### 1. AI answers are taking the easy clicks

Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT and other AI assistants now answer a lot of questions directly on the results page. For simple, informational searches ("what is a VPN", "how does a heat pump work"), many people get their answer and never click through to a website.

That hurts the classic affiliate play of ranking a basic article and collecting clicks. The searches that still send real visitors tend to be the ones where people want a **specific, trustworthy opinion before they spend money**: comparisons, hands-on reviews, "which one should I pick for my situation."

### 2. Big programs cut commissions

Over 2026, Adweek and others reported that Amazon reduced Associates commission rates for many publishers, in some cases by up to half, without a public announcement. Amazon also tightened its program rules in April 2026.

The lesson isn't "never use Amazon." It's that **one program can cut your income overnight, and you have no say.** Relying on a single affiliate program is renting, not owning.

### 3. Google got stricter about thin review content

Google's updates through 2026 continued to push down mass-produced, templated "best X" pages, especially ones written without real experience. In August 2026 Google also revised its policy on low-quality third-party content hosted on strong domains, now called the site reputation policy.

Importantly, Google says affiliate links themselves aren't the problem. Reviews and comparisons are fine when they add something real: original testing, first-hand experience, honest pros and cons.

### 4. Regulators care about fake reviews

The US Federal Trade Commission's rule against fake reviews and testimonials took effect in late 2024, and in late 2025 the FTC started sending warning letters under it. Separately, the FTC has long required that you clearly disclose when you earn money from a recommendation.

For affiliates, that means: **disclose every affiliate link clearly, and never fake or exaggerate experience with a product.**

## What still works in 2027

### Reviews built on real use

The most durable affiliate content is the kind AI can't easily copy: what actually happened when *you* used the thing. Screenshots, what surprised you, what annoyed you, who it's wrong for. This is also exactly what Google says it wants.

### Tools and software over physical products

Software, online platforms and courses often pay recurring commissions (a percentage every month the customer stays) and usually pay more per sale than physical products. If you already use tools to run something online, reviewing those tools honestly is the natural place to start.

### Owning your audience

Search and social reach both go up and down with algorithms. An email list doesn't. The affiliates who weathered 2026 best were the ones who could reach their readers directly with a newsletter instead of waiting for Google to send them back.

### Short-form video as the front door

TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts are now where many people first discover products. A 30-second "here's the tool I actually use for X" video can do what a blog post used to: introduce the product. The difference is what comes next. Send viewers somewhere you control, like an article or a signup, instead of hoping they remember you.

### A small number of programs you trust

Instead of joining twenty programs, pick a handful of products you'd recommend even if they paid nothing. Prefer programs that pay recurring commissions, have reasonable cookie windows (how long after a click a sale still counts) and a track record of treating affiliates fairly.

## How to start affiliate marketing in 2027

1. **Pick a narrow topic you know firsthand.** "Tools for running a one-person online business" beats "technology."
2. **List the products you already use and like.** These are your first reviews. Check which ones have affiliate programs.
3. **Build on land you own.** A simple website for long-form reviews and comparisons, plus an email list from day one.
4. **Use social video to get discovered.** Each article becomes two or three short clips pointing back to it.
5. **Disclose everything.** A plain line at the top of each article: some links are affiliate links, and you may earn a commission at no extra cost to the reader.
6. **Give it time.** New sites usually take months, not weeks, to get meaningful search traffic. Plan for that.

## Is affiliate marketing still worth it?

Yes, if you treat it as building an asset, not finding a loophole. The shortcuts (mass-produced reviews, one big program, pure search traffic) got much weaker in 2026. The fundamentals (real experience, honest recommendations, an audience you own) got relatively stronger, because there's less noise competing with them.
